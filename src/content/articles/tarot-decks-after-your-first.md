Most people buy their first tarot deck to learn on. The second purchase is different: by then you know what you liked and what got in your way. The useful question isn't which deck is best, it's which one fixes something about the deck you already have.

If you haven't started yet, begin with our [tarot for beginners guide](/articles/tarot-for-beginners/), which explains why the Rider-Waite is the deck to learn on.

## Same system, new art

The safest second deck keeps the structure you already know and only changes the pictures. Your card meanings carry over, and the new images often show you something you'd stopped noticing.

The Light Seer's and Modern Witch decks are the two most popular choices, and both follow the traditional cards closely. Light Seer's leans toward gentle, modern characters; Modern Witch is bolder and more contemporary. The Golden Art Nouveau deck is the choice for looks, with the same symbols in gold-foil detail.

## The original drawings

The Rider-Waite most people learn on has been recolored many times. The Smith-Waite Centennial reproduces Pamela Colman Smith's 1909 artwork in her original muted colors, and it's a good way to see where the cards you know came from. The pocket size suits travel more than table reading.

## A deck that asks more of you

The Wild Unknown replaces the familiar scenes with simple drawings of animals and nature. With less story on each card, you rely more on what you know and how the image strikes you. That makes it a better second or third deck than a first.

## A different system entirely

The Thoth deck and the Marseille tradition aren't new art on the same cards; they work differently.

The Thoth deck builds in astrological and Hebrew-letter correspondences, renames several cards, and comes from a different school of thought. Expect to read about it before you read with it.

Marseille decks are older than the Rider-Waite. The numbered minor cards show patterns of suit symbols, not scenes, so you read them through number and suit. People who learned on pictures sometimes find this freeing and sometimes find it hard.

## Keeping more than one deck

Deck boxes are made of thin card and fall apart with use. A small wooden box keeps your main deck together and protected. Check the inside dimensions against your deck's card size, since large decks won't fit standard boxes.

## Worth being direct about

A new deck won't make your readings more accurate, because there's no evidence that tarot predicts anything. What changes is how you react to the images, and that's the real value of tarot: a random picture gives you something to respond to, and your response shows what you were already thinking. A deck you enjoy looking at makes that practice easier to keep up. Buy for the art and the system, not for promises about insight or energy in the listing.

Candle magic is one of the simpler forms of intention-based practice — a single candle, chosen and often dressed (anointed with oil or carved with a symbol) based on what you're focused on, then lit with that intention in mind. Most of its structure comes down to color correspondence: different colors are traditionally associated with different intentions (green for abundance, red for passion or courage, white as an all-purpose substitute for any color you don't have).

**Worth saying plainly:** this practice involves actual open flame. Never leave a burning candle unattended, keep it away from anything flammable, and use a proper heatproof holder — no beginner guide's spiritual instructions override basic fire safety.

## Color is the main organizing system

Before buying a full rainbow of candles, it's worth learning what each color traditionally represents — most systems agree on the big ones (green for money/abundance, pink for self-love, black for banishing or protection) with some variation at the edges. A single foundational book covers this clearly enough that a large set of colored candles becomes genuinely useful rather than a guessing game.

## White substitutes for any color you don't have

A common practical rule: a white candle can stand in for any other color when the specific one isn't available, since white is treated as containing all colors. This is worth knowing before assuming a spell is impossible just because the "correct" color candle isn't on hand.

## Dressing a candle is optional, not required

Anointing a candle with oil or carving a symbol or word into the wax ("dressing" it) is a common intensifying step, but a plain, undressed candle lit with clear intention still counts as a genuine practice — the dressing step is an addition, not a requirement for it to "count."

## Put the flame out properly, and on something that won't burn

Two cheap tools do most of the safety work. A snuffer puts a candle out without blowing on it, and a heatproof holder stops a burning stub from touching anything else. The Wyspell set includes six holders for 36 candles, so if you burn several at once you'll want more of both.

## Timing is a real variable worth knowing about

Some practitioners tie candle spells to moon phases or specific days of the week associated with different intentions (Friday for love, for instance). This is a layer worth learning once the basics — color, intention, safety — are already comfortable, not something to worry about on a first attempt.

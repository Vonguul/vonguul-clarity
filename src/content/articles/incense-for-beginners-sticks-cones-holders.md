Incense is one of the oldest ways to mark a space as different from the everyday: lighting a stick before meditation or a ritual is a small signal to yourself that this time is set apart. It's also inexpensive, and it's easy to get started badly, with ash all over the table and a scent you don't like.

## Find your scents first

Start with a variety pack rather than a big box of one scent. You'll quickly find you like a few and dislike most, and those few are worth buying in quantity.

## Indian or Japanese

Most incense sold in the US is Indian-style: a scented paste rolled onto a bamboo stick, strong and sweet, like Nag Champa. Japanese incense has no stick in the middle. It's thinner, usually gives off less smoke, and has a quieter scent that suits a small room or a long session. Try both before deciding.

## The holder matters more than you'd think

Ash is the usual complaint, and the holder is the fix. An angled wooden tray catches it under the stick. A hanging holder inside a glass tube catches all of it, which also helps with pets and drafts. Backflow burners are mostly about the look: smoke flowing down over ceramic. They need their own cones with a hole in the base.

## Worth being direct about

Burning incense is burning something indoors, so it produces smoke and fine particles like a candle or a fireplace does. Open a window or ventilate the room, keep it well away from children, pets and anything that can catch, never leave it burning unattended, and skip it around anyone with asthma or breathing problems. Claims that incense "cleanses energy" aren't something that can be tested. Treat it as a ritual cue and a scent you enjoy.

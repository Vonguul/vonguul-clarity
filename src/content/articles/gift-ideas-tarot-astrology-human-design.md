The hard part of buying for someone into tarot, astrology or Human Design is that it's easy to buy the wrong level. A beginner's deck for someone who's read for years, or an expensive reference book for someone who has only just found out their Type. The gifts below are grouped by where the person actually is, so you can match the gift to them.

Everything here comes from our individual guides to each practice, where each product was picked for a specific reason. We've left prices out because Amazon's move around a lot during the holidays.

## Tarot: a first deck, or something for the experienced reader

If they're curious but don't own a deck, the original Rider-Waite imagery is the safe choice, since it's what nearly every book teaches from. A tarot journal pairs with it, and a cloth and pouch make an easy add-on.

If they already read regularly, skip another standard deck. A spreads book gives them new ways to use the cards they have, and a distinctive second deck adds something new rather than duplicating what they own.

## The moon person

The custom moon-phase necklace is the most personal gift on this list: it shows the real phase of the moon on a date you choose, like their birthday. A moon lamp and a lunar-cycle journal are good additional or alternative choices.

## Astrology and Human Design

For astrology, a well-illustrated reference book suits anyone who knows their sun sign and wants to learn their full chart.

For Human Design, the big reference books are exactly the kind of thing people wish-list rather than buy for themselves. Before choosing, find out which teacher they follow: the Definitive Book is the original system, and the Encyclopedia of Quantum Human Design follows Karen Curry Parker's approach. If they're brand new to Human Design, a book isn't the best first gift. Send them to a free chart site first, and see our Human Design guide for where to start.

## Meditation, journaling, and crystals

A singing bowl set is a good gift for someone who meditates because it's easy to play well on the first try. The Five Minute Journal suits anyone into intention-setting. For crystals, the standard reference book is a safer gift than stones, since collectors tend to have strong opinions about which ones they want.

## Runes and dreams

A complete rune set with a guidebook needs nothing else to go with it. For someone into lucid dreaming, a contoured sleep mask and LaBerge's book make a good pair, and the mask is useful whatever they think about dreams.

## The curious skeptic

Some gifts work for both the believer and the skeptic: astronomy binoculars that show the real moon and planets, a geology guide to the stones in a crystal collection, and Jung's own introduction to the symbolism that modern tarot draws on.

## Worth being direct about

There's no scientific evidence that tarot, astrology or Human Design predict events or describe personality better than chance. That doesn't make them a bad gift. People get real value from them as tools for reflection, and a thoughtful gift supports something they already enjoy. We've chosen items that are well made and useful on their own terms, and included a few, like the binoculars, geology guide and Jung, that are interesting whatever someone believes.

## A note on timing

The moon-phase necklace is made to order, and holiday shipping gets slower from mid-November. Ordering personalized items in October avoids the rush.

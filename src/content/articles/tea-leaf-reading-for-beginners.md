Tea leaf reading, or tasseography, is reading the shapes left by loose tea leaves at the bottom of a cup. It became a popular parlor pastime in the 1800s and early 1900s, and it's one of the most low-key forms of divination: a cup of tea with a few minutes of reflection at the end.

## Start with what you have

Any loose tea and any plain, wide, light-colored cup will do. A white cup with sloping sides makes the leaves easiest to see.

1. Put a pinch of loose tea in the cup and pour hot water over it. Don't use a strainer: the leaves need to stay in the cup.
2. Drink the tea slowly, leaving a small amount of liquid at the bottom. Traditionally, this is when you think about the question you want to ask.
3. Swirl the cup three times, turn it upside down onto the saucer, and leave it for a minute so the liquid drains.
4. Turn the cup back up and look at the shapes the leaves have formed.

Readers usually treat the handle as your position, so leaves near it are about you and the near future. The rim is the present, and the bottom of the cup is further off.

## Reading the shapes

Look at the shapes first, before checking any meanings. Do you see an animal, a letter, a line, a cluster? Write down what you see and what it brings to mind. Then look it up. The traditional symbol meanings are where the cards and books come in, but many modern guides, including Reading the Leaves, put your own first impression ahead of the dictionary.

## Worth being direct about

Tea leaves don't contain information about your future. The shapes are random, and the brain's habit of finding faces and figures in random patterns, called pareidolia, is what turns them into a bird or a ring. The traditional symbol meanings were mostly written down by Victorian and Edwardian fortune-tellers, not handed down from an ancient source.

The practice is still a pleasant way to reflect: what you decide a shape means says a lot about what's on your mind. If you enjoy this, [scrying for beginners](/articles/scrying-for-beginners/) works on the same principle with a mirror or a crystal ball.

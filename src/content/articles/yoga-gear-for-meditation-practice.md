Most people who give up meditating give up because sitting still hurts: stiff hips, sore knees, a back that gives out after ten minutes. Our [meditation guide](/articles/meditation-for-beginners/) covers cushions and benches. This guide covers the other half of the answer: a few minutes of gentle yoga before you sit, which is what the postures were originally for.

## Why yoga and meditation go together

In the Yoga Sutras, the physical postures are one step on a path that leads to concentration and meditation. Practically, gentle hip openers and forward folds loosen the joints that complain during a long sit. Ten minutes on a mat before a session is often the difference between sitting for ten minutes and sitting for thirty.

## Choose a mat by what you'll do on it

- **Mostly sitting, kneeling and floor stretches:** a thick, soft mat like the Gaiam is the most comfortable and costs the least.
- **Standing poses as well:** a firmer mat like the Manduka PRO stays stable under your feet and still cushions your knees.
- **If you want help with alignment:** the Liforme's printed guide lines help you set up the same way every time.

## The three props worth having

Blocks, a strap and a bolster let you do the poses with support instead of forcing them. A block under the hips is also an instant meditation seat, and a bolster turns the end of a session into a few minutes of lying still.

## Books

*Light on Yoga* is the reference for the poses. *Light on the Yoga Sutras* explains where the poses fit in the larger practice, and it's the better read if your interest is meditation. If you want a framework for what you notice while sitting, our [chakras guide](/articles/chakras-for-beginners/) offers one.

## Worth being direct about

Research on yoga is strongest for modest improvements in flexibility, balance and how people rate their own stress. It isn't a treatment for any medical condition, and the pain-relief claims on many strap and bolster listings are the makers' own. If you have an injury, back problems, or are pregnant, ask a doctor or a qualified teacher which poses to avoid. Stop any pose that causes sharp pain. Mat thickness and size figures are the makers'.

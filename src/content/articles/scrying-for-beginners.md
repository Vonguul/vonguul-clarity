Scrying is gazing into a surface, traditionally a dark mirror, a crystal ball or still water, in dim light, until images or impressions seem to form. It's one of the oldest divination practices, and it needs less equipment than almost anything else on this site.

## Start with a bowl of water

You don't need to buy anything to find out whether scrying suits you. Fill a dark bowl with water, or add a few drops of black ink to a light one. Sit in a dim room with a single candle placed behind you or to the side, so its flame isn't reflected in the surface. Let your eyes relax and your gaze soften for ten or fifteen minutes. Write down whatever you notice, without judging it.

If that holds your interest after a few sessions, a black mirror or a crystal ball gives you a dedicated tool that's more consistent than water.

## Mirror or ball?

A black obsidian mirror is the simpler of the two. It's dark, so there's little to distract you, and the reflection seems to fade into depth as your eyes relax. A crystal ball does the opposite: it's clear, and people describe the gazing as looking into it, often with the ball set against a dark cloth. Neither is better. Most people find they prefer one after trying both, and the inexpensive glass ball is the cheapest way to find out.

## Glass, reconstituted quartz and natural quartz

Crystal ball listings blur this, so it's worth knowing the difference. A glass ball is just that. A reconstituted quartz ball is real quartz that has been melted and re-formed, which makes it flawlessly clear. A natural quartz sphere is cut from a single crystal, usually has visible inclusions, and costs much more at any size worth gazing into. For the practice itself, they behave the same: you're looking at light in a clear sphere.

## Worth being direct about

There's no controlled evidence that scrying shows the future or anything outside your own mind. What it reliably produces has a well-studied explanation. Staring at a uniform, featureless field in dim light leads the visual system to generate images of its own: this is known as the Ganzfeld effect. The brain's habit of finding faces and shapes in random patterns, called pareidolia, does the rest. That's why the images feel real: they're coming from you.

That doesn't make scrying pointless. Taken as a focus exercise, it's close to meditation, and what you "see" can be a useful look at what's on your mind.

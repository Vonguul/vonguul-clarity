The chakras are a map of seven points along the body, from the base of the spine to the crown of the head, each linked to an area of life. You'll see them on yoga posters, crystal sets and meditation apps. Used as a checklist for how different parts of your life are going, the map can be a useful prompt. As a claim about anatomy, it doesn't hold up.

## The seven, briefly

In the version most people meet today:

1. **Root** (base of the spine): safety, stability, the basics.
2. **Sacral** (lower belly): pleasure, emotion, creativity.
3. **Solar plexus** (upper belly): will, confidence, getting things done.
4. **Heart** (center of the chest): love, connection, compassion.
5. **Throat**: speaking and being heard.
6. **Third eye** (between the brows): intuition and insight.
7. **Crown** (top of the head): meaning and connection to something larger.

## Start with a notebook, not a crystal set

You don't need to buy anything to try this. Take the list above and, once a week, write a line for each chakra about that area of your life right now. Where do you feel steady? Where is something stuck? After a month, read it back and look for patterns. That's the useful core of the practice, and it costs nothing.

If you meditate, you can also move your attention slowly from the base of the spine to the crown, pausing at each point. Our [meditation guide](/articles/meditation-for-beginners/) covers how to sit comfortably for long enough to do it.

## Which book first

Marion McGeough's short guide is the gentlest start. Margarita Alcantara's book adds exercises for each chakra. Anodea Judith's *Wheels of Life* is the standard reference, and her *Eastern Body, Western Mind* is the one to read if you want the system explained in terms of psychology rather than energy.

## Worth being direct about

There is no anatomical or measured evidence that chakras exist as energy centers in the body. The idea comes from older Indian tantric and yogic traditions, but the version on most posters, seven chakras each with a rainbow color and a list of personality traits, was largely shaped by Western writers in the 20th century. The traditional texts don't agree on a single fixed list.

None of that stops the map being useful as a structure for reflection. Treat a "blocked" chakra as a prompt to look at that area of your life, never as a diagnosis. Chakra work isn't a treatment for any physical or mental health condition, and crystal sets sold as "chakra healing" can't do what their listings claim.

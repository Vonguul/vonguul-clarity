## Angle

Many of the practices on this site (tarot, candles, crystals, meditation, the Wheel of the Year) assume a place to do them, and
beginners either skip it or buy a pile of decor. The article treats an altar as a practical space: a surface, something to protect
it, a safe place for a flame or incense, somewhere to keep the tools between sessions, and nothing more. Traditions differ on what
an altar means; the article describes them as reflection and ritual spaces, not as anything that works by itself.

**Why now:** Yule (December) is when many readers set up a seasonal altar, and it links the sabbat series (Yule live, Imbolc and
Ostara scheduled, Beltane researched). A spin-off of `wheel-of-the-year-yule-for-beginners`, which already lists an altar cloth.

## Writing notes

- "Worth being direct about" section: an altar is a focus and a habit, not a mechanism. Nothing on it has a proven effect; it gives a
  practice a place, which makes the practice easier to keep. Note that traditions (Wicca, folk practice, Buddhist and Hindu home shrines)
  differ, and the article doesn't claim to speak for any of them.
- Fire safety in its own section: never leave a flame unattended, keep it off cloth and away from curtains, use a tray under every
  candle and incense, ventilate.
- Keep it small: one surface, one cloth, one tray, one box. The point is fewer things, kept in one place.
- No prices.
